ChoralRoot 0.22-beta1 is a beta of 0.22: a sixth engine, P5, 128 sound slots shared by all engines, favorites, FM TONE
in the Digitone's own ranges with Digitone sounds to import, and knobs that accelerate. It is opt-in: the web
installer offers it only when you tick **Show beta releases** under Other versions, and 0.21 stays its default. A beta
may have bugs (please report them on GitHub), and the next stable release, 0.22, replaces it. Updating from 0.21 keeps
your settings, sounds and loops; going back to 0.21 is possible at any time (the last section says what it means).

## Sounds and engines

- **A new engine: P5**, the Prophet-5 model of Melodee (Kerem Kilic's FM-1 firmware): two oscillators with sync and
  Poly-Mod, the four-pole filter in its Rev 1/2 and Rev 3 characters, two envelopes and the wheel's modulation. It
  comes with 115 of the 200 factory programs Sequential made for the Prophet-5 (picked by ear), after `00 INIT`.
  Choose it as any engine: hold OPT (printed GLO) and turn PRESETS for the chord part or ALGORITHM for the bass. EDIT opens its pages: the two oscillators, the mixer, the filter, both envelopes, the LFO, the wheel,
  Poly-Mod and the voice (UNISON, voices, detune, glide, priority, Vintage).
  - **UNISON.** A program whose UNISON switch is on loads in UNISON: one note on up to four stacked voices (a chord
    plays its lowest or its last note, as the program's key priority says). The next program without it plays chords
    again.
  - **Voices.** With Dual Core on (the new default, below) the chord part plays up to eight P5 voices, shared between
    the chip's two cores. A P5 bass gets up to four (a UNISON stack), but the chord comes first, and the bass always
    keeps one voice. With one core P5 plays four voices, three while a P5 bass sounds, and a P5 bass plays one. When
    you hold more keys than there are voices, the oldest held note that is not the lowest fades out quickly to make
    room.
  - **Level.** P5 plays a little quieter than the other engines (a four-note chord about 4 dB under FM6 and 6 dB under
    the VA), so that UNISON programs and big chords stay clear of the output's limiter.
- **FM TONE in the Digitone's ranges.** FM TONE's values now read like the Digitone's, so a sound can be compared and
  copied number for number: FREQ (the cutoff, now the Digitone's number instead of Hz), RESO, BASE and DTUNE from 0.00
  to 127.00 with two decimals, HARM from -26.00 to +26.00, the filter envelope's DEPTH and each LFO's SPEED and DEPTH
  from -64.00 to +63.99, FDBK from 0 to 127, LEVEL a whole number from 0 to 127, and AMP DEC and AMP REL whole numbers
  from 0 to 126, then INF. AMP DEC at INF holds the level the attack reached, as the Digitone's DEC 127 does (AMP REL's
  INF is as before). VEL, the operators' KEY and the filter's SUS and KTRK read 0 to 127 instead of a percentage.
- **FM TONE sounds from before 0.22 are converted** when they load (your saved sounds, the factory sounds, the last
  session, backups and sound files): each value is carried over into its new range. The conversion is close, not
  exact, so a sound may come out a little different; an AMP DEC that was at its top (127) is INF now, so the sound
  holds its level instead of falling slowly to SUS. The next save keeps the sound in the new form.
- **FM TONE's LFOs reach all 57 of the Digitone's destinations**, listed in the Digitone's order on the DEST cell. New
  are LFO 1's own SPEED, MULT, FADE, WAVE, start phase, MODE and DEPTH (from LFO 2 and 3), RATIO ALL, A and B together
  (LEV, ATK, DEC, END, DLY), DRIVE, the reverb, delay and chorus sends, and the filter envelope's DELAY.

- **Digitone sounds into FM TONE.** The device manager takes sound files exported from an Elektron Digitone (`.dnsnd`,
  several at once) and turns each into an FM TONE sound that you place in a slot like any other; the installer page's
  Sounds section imports them one at a time. The files are read on your computer: nothing is uploaded. The algorithm,
  the ratios, the operators' envelopes and levels, the filter and its type, the amp envelope, the LFOs and their
  destinations, DRIVE, the decimals and, where it can, the sound's octave are carried over; what does not translate,
  or only roughly, is listed beside each sound before you place it. An imported sound is close to the Digitone's, not
  the same: the file format was worked out from exported files, and the times and levels are approximations.
- **Replace the factory sounds in one go.** In the device manager, **Replace factory** puts the sounds you selected in
  the library (Digitone imports or any other sounds of one engine) in the places of that engine's factory presets, in
  order: PRESETS then steps through your sounds where the factory ones were. Each takes a user slot; more sounds than
  factory presets are added after them while free slots last. **Restore factory order** brings the factory presets
  back and keeps your sounds as added ones. The factory sounds stay in the firmware either way.
- **VA: a finer filter envelope and more sync rates.** The filter envelope's depth (FENV) has two decimals, -64.00 to
  +63.99, so a small amount such as 0.04 is possible. A synced LFO's RATE is a list of 42 rates: 8, 4, 3, 2 and 1
  bars and 1/2 to 1/256, each dotted, straight and triplet, then 4, 3 and 2 ms. It moves one rate a detent; with OPT
  held it steps the straight ones. VA sounds from before 0.22 are converted exactly and sound as they did.

## Playing and editing

- **Chord numerals in Key Mode.** A new setting, **Numerals**, on the KEY layer's second page (hold KEY, turn SELECT,
  KNOB 4). It is off until you turn it on. With Key Mode on, the Chord view then shows the Roman numeral of the chord
  you are playing, in blue above the chord's name: in C major `I` for C, `V` for G, `vi` for Am. Capitals are chords
  with a major third, small letters chords with a minor third. The numeral is the degree you played: Transpose is
  left out, so the tonic chord reads `I` at any Transpose, while the name shows the chord you hear. Hold SAVE to keep
  the setting. Nothing you hear changes.
- **The knobs accelerate.** A value knob turned slowly moves its finest step (1, or 0.01 on a value with decimals);
  turned quickly it moves up to a twentieth of the range a detent, so a quick turn of about one revolution crosses
  the whole range. That is every value in the sound editor of every engine and most value knobs outside it (the
  effects, the BASS layer's level, Options > Velocity). Lists (presets, algorithms, named choices, the VA's sync
  rates) still move one item a detent, and so do the tempo, the PLAY menu's Level and the click level.
- **Hold OPT to snap.** While you hold OPT (printed GLO), a value knob jumps to round values: 0, 16, 32 .. 127 on a 0
  to 127 value, steps of 8 on -64 to 63, whole numbers on a value with decimals, 8, 16, 32 and 64 on an LFO speed,
  octaves on a value in semitones. In the sound editor GLO is SHIFT: hold it, or tap it to keep snapping until you
  tap it again (`snap` shows in the title line). Until 0.21 SHIFT gave fine steps in the editor; a slow turn does that
  now. OPT's other jobs (with PRESETS, ALGORITHM, KNOB 1 and SELECT) are unchanged.
- **Dual Core is on by default.** A new FM-1, and one whose settings you reset (Options > Reset Settings), now uses
  the chip's second core: the bass plays there with its own voices, and P5's chords get their eight voices. **Updating
  does not change your setting**: if your FM-1 had Dual Core Off, it still has. To turn it on, set Options > Dual Core
  to On and switch the FM-1 off and on. If your FM-1 ever crashes or restarts while playing, set Dual Core to Off and
  switch off and on. Should the second core keep the FM-1 from starting, the next start comes up on one core with
  Dual Core reading `On · restart`, so that you can switch it Off there.

## Saving and favorites

- **128 user sound slots, shared by all engines.** Save up to 128 of your own sounds, of any engine, in any mix (until
  now 32). The slots are numbered U001 to U128. Your 32 sounds from 0.21 are copied into U001 to U032 the first time
  the FM-1 starts with 0.22, each with its own patch. The device manager and the installer page's Sounds section work
  with the 128 slots, and with a 0.21 FM-1's 32 as before.
- **P5 sounds can be saved.** SAVE on a P5 sound offers Overwrite and Save as new like every engine: the whole program
  is kept with the sound, edited or not, and loading it brings back exactly what you saved, with its UNISON. The
  device manager exports and imports P5 sounds as sound files.
- **Naming a sound has a cursor.** When you Save as new, SELECT moves a cursor along the name (turned right on the
  last letter it adds one), KNOB 1 changes the letter under it and KNOB 4 to the left deletes it, so a name can be
  written with the knobs alone. The keys still type, just after the cursor. The other knobs do nothing while you name
  a sound: PRESETS and ALGORITHM no longer change the sound under the name, and KNOB 2 no longer changes the last
  letter (that is KNOB 1 at the cursor).
- **Favorites, with a filter.** Mark the sounds you like, then step through just those. Tap SAVE: in the save menu
  **KNOB 4 marks the sound** (right) or takes the mark off (left); nothing is saved or overwritten. Or mark while you
  browse: in the sound editor turn PRESETS and use KNOB 4 on the sound you are hearing. A marked sound shows a small
  yellow star before its name when you turn PRESETS or ALGORITHM. Factory presets, P5's programs and your own sounds
  can be marked (`00 INIT` cannot).
  - **The filter.** In the save menu **KNOB 2** turns the favorites filter on or off. While it is on, PRESETS and
    ALGORITHM step through the favorites of the engine you are on and nothing else; a yellow line under the meter
    (`favorites 03/07`) shows that it is on. An engine without a favorite steps through all its sounds and says `no
    favorites: all`.
  - **Chord or bass.** **KNOB 3** gives a sound a type: `Both`, `Chord` or `Bass`. With the filter on, Chord sounds
    are offered to the chord part only and Bass sounds to the bass part only. The type stays with the sound when you
    take its mark off and put it back.
  - **The save menu** now works like the Options: SELECT picks one of five entries (Overwrite, Save as new, Favorite,
    Favorites filter, Favorite type) and KNOB 1 changes the selected entry's value. KNOB 1 no longer moves between
    Overwrite and Save as new: use SELECT.
  - The marks, the types and the filter are kept by themselves and are part of a backup. With the command-line tool,
    `--favorites` prints them as text and `--restore-favorites` writes such a text back.
- **Fewer pauses when the FM-1 writes to flash.** A flash write used to stop the sound for about 18 ms. The FM-1 now
  clears the space ahead of time, at power-on or after ten seconds of quiet, for your favorites, for what SAVE held
  keeps (the sounds and the state you come back to) and for the 128 slots, so those writes no longer pause the sound
  (a second favorite change soon after waits for a quiet moment; a second save soon after pauses as before).
  Changing an Option still pauses once, after you stop playing, as in 0.21, and so does saving a loop. Taking a
  backup, or reading the favorites from a computer, no longer writes anything to the FM-1.
- **Back up before you try another firmware.** Some other FM-1 firmwares (Melodee, a remix of ChoralRoot) use the part
  of the flash where 0.22 keeps your favorites and your sounds. A full backup from the installer page or the device
  manager keeps everything; restore it when you come back.

## Installing

- **A beta, opt-in.** In the web installer tick **Show beta releases** under Other versions (the page remembers it in
  that browser), choose 0.22-beta1 and press Install. Without the tick the installer offers 0.21, the latest stable
  release, as before. On an FM-1 that runs the beta, "What is on my FM-1?" says so, and Install then writes 0.21 unless
  you choose the beta again. Options > Version reads `ChoralRoot 0.22-beta1`.
- **A Single core package, for FM-1s that have trouble with Dual Core.** The web installer offers it under
  **Advanced > Hardware fallback**: the same firmware, built so that the second core is never started. Choose it if
  your FM-1 crashes or freezes with the standard package. Options > Dual Core then reads `1 core build`, and the
  version `ChoralRoot 0.22-beta1 1-core` (Options > Version shows it as `0.22-beta1 1-core`, the line being too short
  for the whole name). Your settings, sounds, loops and favorites stay when you switch between the two.

## Going back to 0.21

The installer offers 0.21 at any time: it is the default. 0.21 has none of the above, and:

- **Your sounds.** 0.21 shows its 32 slots as they were when you updated: 0.22 never writes them. Sounds saved under
  0.22, in any slot, are not there; they are still in the 128 slots when you come back. Sounds you save under 0.21 are
  not copied by themselves when you return: the device manager's **Import the 0.21 slots** copies 0.21's 32 slots into
  U001 to U032 again, over what is there (U033 to U128 stay).
- **A sound saved under 0.22, taken to 0.21** (export it with the device manager, then place it on 0.21): P5 sounds
  cannot go, 0.21 has no P5. The device manager converts FM TONE and VA sounds to what 0.21 reads and says what
  changed: values with decimals are rounded, an FM TONE LFO aimed at one of the destinations new in 0.22 is switched
  off, an AMP DEC at INF becomes 0.21's longest decay, and a VA LFO synced to a rate 0.21 does not have takes the
  nearest one it has.
- **The sound you left playing.** A part left on a P5 sound starts on 0.21's default sound. 0.21 does not read FM TONE
  and VA sounds in their 0.22 form either: a part left on one may start on another sound, or on INIT.
- **Favorites.** 0.21 has none: it ignores the marks, the types and the filter, and leaves them alone, so they are
  still there when you come back.
- **Numerals.** 0.21 has no Numerals setting and ignores it; nothing else is lost or reset. If you hold SAVE while on
  0.21, Numerals is off when you come back.
- **Dual Core and the single-core package.** 0.21 keeps the Dual Core setting as it is (On on an FM-1 that was new or
  reset under 0.22) and has no single-core package: on an FM-1 that needs one core, set Options > Dual Core to Off on
  0.21 straight after installing it.
- **The knobs** move in fixed steps again, and SHIFT in the sound editor gives fine steps again.
- **Backups.** A backup made with 0.22 holds the 128 slots and the favorites; restored on 0.21 it brings back the
  settings, the FM6 and CZ-1 banks and the loops, but not the sounds or the favorites. A backup made with 0.21 restores
  on 0.22 into U001 to U032.
