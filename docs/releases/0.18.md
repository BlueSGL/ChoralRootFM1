ChoralRoot 0.18 (in progress)

## Sounds

**Six engines instead of eleven.** PHASE, LOFI, VOICE, TRIO and NOISE are retired. They came with Felucca, the
firmware ChoralRoot is built on, and were never part of what ChoralRoot is; their place is wanted for new effects.
The engines are now FM6, FM TONE, VA, CZ-1, WHEEL and PHYS, in that order in the engine picker (SELECT steps them).
Their 24 factory presets go with them.

If you saved sounds on one of the five:

- Nothing is deleted. Each sound stays in its slot under its name, and a backup or the device manager still holds
  it exactly as you saved it.
- It no longer sounds as it did. You find it at the end of the VA's presets. Picking it plays the VA's plain
  starting sound with your sound's effect sends, attack and release, and the screen says `PHASE retired: stand-in`
  (or LOFI, VOICE, TRIO, NOISE). Edit it from there and save it to keep a VA sound in its place.
- The device manager shows it in the VA's tab as `PHASE · retired`; the installer page's Sounds table and
  `fm1_install.py --sounds` list it with its engine and `retired`.
- If the chord sound or the bass sound was on one of these engines when you update, it comes up on FM6 `TINE EP`
  or VA `DEEP SUB`, as on a new unit. Your other settings are kept.
- A backup made on 0.17 restores on 0.18 without error, with the same result.

Going back: on 0.17 these sounds play on their own engines again, as long as you did not save over them.

## Effects

**A new reverb and a new delay.** The old reverb (Room or Spring) and the old single echo are replaced.

- **The reverb** is one large, slow space. Size takes it from a hall to something far bigger, Decay from 0.3 s to
  72 s and, at the top, `Inf`: the reverb holds what is in it until you turn Decay down. The tail moves slowly
  inside (Mod) and never rings. New controls: Decay, Pre-delay, Mod, Low cut (keeps the bass out of it), Width.
- **The delay** is stereo, after the Moog Matriarch's: a time for the left side and a Spread that makes the right
  side a part of it (synced: 1:1, 3:4, 2:3, 1:2; free: down to half), Stereo or Ping-pong, synced to the tempo with
  dotted and triplet values or free in milliseconds, up to three quarters of a second a side. Its repeats are as
  bright as the note at first; each one is darker than the last (Tone), with a slow Wow if you want it. Changing
  the time or the tempo bends the pitch of the repeats for a moment, like a tape echo, and never clicks. Turned up,
  Feedback keeps a short echo ringing for many seconds and a long one for minutes: a tail dies away evenly and is
  never cut.
- **The FX layer has six pages** on SELECT: Reverb 1, Reverb 2, Chorus, Delay 1, Delay 2, Drive. The small line
  above the effect's name shows the page (`fx · on · 1/2`). Seven new pictures on the cells.
- **The effects and their sends belong to the instrument, not to the sound.** The reverb's, the chorus's and the
  delay's settings are set once for the whole FM-1. So are the sends: the chord part has one send into each effect
  and its drive (KNOB 4 on an effect's first page, named `Send`), the bass part has its own (in the bass sound's
  editor, FX group). **Turning PRESETS, changing the engine or loading a saved sound changes none of it**: step
  through the sounds and hear each one in the same reverb and delay. Everything is back when you switch on.
- **FX off is off for the bass too.** Until 0.17 a bass sound with a reverb send kept its reverb with FX off.
- The chorus and the drive are as they were.

What you will notice after updating:

- The reverb and the delay **start at their new defaults** (a 3 s reverb, a 1/8 ping-pong delay). What you had set
  for the old ones (size, damp, type; time, feedback, colour) is not carried over: the old values mean nothing to
  the new effects. The chorus's settings and all saved sounds are unchanged.
- **Sounds no longer bring their own sends.** Until 0.17 every preset and every saved sound set the four sends when
  you picked it; now the sends stay where you put them. The chord part starts with the sends of the power-on sound
  (reverb 35, chorus 45, delay 25, no drive) or with the ones you last had; the bass with a little reverb. A sound
  you saved for its heavy reverb loads with the reverb you have now: set the Send once. The send values inside
  saved sounds are still there (nothing is rewritten), they are just not used. MIDI: CC 91 / 93 / 94 set the
  part's sends, and a program change no longer resets them.
- **The same send gives the same loudness as before**: of the reverb under a held chord, and of each echo
  (measured, within half a decibel). The reverb tail is longer than the old room's.
- Once the tails have died away the effects cost nothing. Sounding, they need more of the processor than the old
  ones did.
- **Long tails and switching off.** The FM-1 stores your settings when it is quiet, because storing stops the sound
  for a moment. A ringing reverb or echo is not quiet, so nothing is stored while a tail rings: with Decay at `Inf`
  not until you turn it down or tap FX off. Let the sound end and wait a few seconds before you switch off.

**In the engine picker the keys always play.** Hold EDIT (or turn PRESETS in the editor): SELECT steps the engines,
KNOB 1 the presets, and the root keys and chord keys play, so that you hear each sound as you turn to it. Until
0.17 the white root keys chose the engine there unless KNOB 4 switched them to playing; that switch is gone, KNOB 4
does nothing in the picker.

Going back to 0.17: its own effects are there again with their defaults, sounds bring their own sends again (a sound
saved on 0.18 carries the sends the part had when you saved it), and the engine picker's roots play until you turn
its KNOB 4.

## The looper

**A loop keeps your bass line on the bass.** With Single = `Split Bass` the keys below the split play the bass
part's sound. Until now the looper recorded such a note as an ordinary note, so the loop played your bass line back
with the chord sound. Now a note played on the bass side is recorded as a bass note and the loop plays it on the
bass part: the bass sound, and the bass channel on MIDI out.

- Which side a note is on is decided when you record it, and kept. Set Single back to Full, move the split point,
  change the keyboard between Chords and Full or switch the bass off while the loop plays: its bass notes stay on
  the bass, its other notes stay on the chord sound. A note recorded on the chord side never moves to the bass
  because you turn Split Bass on later.
- It holds in every record mode (Overwrite, Advance, Overdub, Replace, Step: a bass-side key entered as a step is a
  bass step), through undo and quantize, in a saved slot, after switching off, and in a backup.
- The bass register (KNOB 2 on the view) moves a looped bass line by octaves, as it moves the bass keys. The chord
  voicing and Transpose do not change it; the loop holds the notes as you played them.
- You can play bass by hand over a looped bass line. The bass part is one voice, so the note played last is the
  one you hear, as with two keys; no note is left hanging when either ends.
- With Split Bass off and the bass switched on while such a loop plays, the bass follows the loop's chords and the
  recorded bass notes play as well, on the same single voice.
- A looped bass note glows on the keys of its note name like the loop's other notes.

Loops recorded with 0.17 or earlier are unchanged: bass-side notes in them were stored as ordinary notes and keep
the chord sound. Record them again to have them on the bass.

Going back: a loop recorded with 0.18 loads on 0.14 to 0.17 and plays there with its bass-side notes on the chord
sound, as those versions recorded them. If the older version saves that loop again (it does when a changed loop
stops), the bass side is lost from it. The size of a loop in memory and in a slot has not changed.

## Switch off, switch on: where you left off

Until now the FM-1 forgot a good part of what you were doing when you switched it off. It now comes back as you
left it. New in what it brings back:

- **Both sounds as you left them, with changes you had not saved.** An edit is no longer lost at power-off. (It is
  still lost when you turn PRESETS to another sound: SAVE keeps it for good.)
- **The parts' Level and Pan**, and so the bass level.
- **The bass being on.** Until 0.17 the bass was off at every power-on; that rule is gone. On a new unit it is off.
- **The voicing** (KNOB 1) **and the octave shift.**
- **LOCK and the chord it latched**: the same type and extensions are lit when you switch on.
- **The effects in full**: the page you had picked, every setting of the reverb, the chorus and the delay (see
  Effects above) and both parts' sends as you left them.

As before: everything in Options, Key Mode and the KEY layer's settings, the performance mode and whether it is on,
LATCH, the tempo and the metronome, the bass behaviour and register, the view, the looper's settings, and the loop
slot.

On purpose it does **not** bring back: notes that were sounding, a playing loop (its slot is loaded, stopped: the
FM-1 never starts playing by itself), a take you were recording, an open layer, menu or the editor (it starts on the
view). After a PANIC it comes back as the PANIC left it.

All of this is stored by itself, a moment after the instrument falls quiet: about two seconds without a change and
without a sound, and never while a loop plays. Give it a few seconds of silence before you switch off. Turning a
knob for a while is one write, after you stop.

**Options > Reset Settings** clears it with the settings: afterwards the FM-1 is as a new unit is. So does SAFE
MODE's Flash Data.

**Backups** are unchanged: a backup holds the settings, your saved sounds and your loops, not the momentary state
above. After restoring a backup the FM-1 starts from the restored settings.

**Going back to 0.17**: it reads 0.18's settings as its own (every option, the calibration), starts as 0.17 always
did (bass off, voicing 0, saved sounds), and ignores the rest. If you return to 0.18 after 0.17 has stored a setting,
0.18 starts from 0.17's settings and not from its own older state.

For the technically minded: the options stay in the 192-byte settings record (now version 15, same size and layout,
which is why 0.17 and the backup tools read it unchanged); the rest is a new 1024-byte session record in two flash
sectors of its own (0xB4000 / 0xB5000), with 143 bytes of reserve and an effects block stored by tag, which is how
the new delay and reverb and the bass part's sends came in without a new format (21 new tags; the six of the old
effects are retired).

## Update mode: OCT-, OCT+ and HOME

**Hold OCT-, OCT+ and HOME for five seconds** to put the FM-1 into update mode while it runs. The two OCT keys do
their PANIC as they go down; with all three held, a red countdown starts after two seconds (`update mode: let go to
cancel`), and at five the FM-1 restarts into update mode: three short flashes, a black screen. Open the installer
and press Install, or switch off and on to start ChoralRoot again; nothing was written. Letting go of any of the
three before the end cancels, and nothing else happens (no HOME, no octave step). It works in SAFE MODE too.

- **OCT- and OCT+ alone no longer do anything when held** beyond their PANIC. Until 0.17, holding the two for five
  seconds was the gesture: too easy to hit by accident.
- **It is real update mode now.** The old gesture went to the chip's own USB boot (the `WL82 UBOOT1.00` disk), which
  the installer cannot use, although the screen said update mode. The new one hands over to the update loader, the
  same way OCT- held at power-on does, so the web installer and `fm1_install.py` find the FM-1 and install.
- What you were doing is stored before the FM-1 leaves and is back afterwards (above).
- On an FM-1 whose ChoralRoot was not installed by ChoralRoot's own installer since 0.16 (no boot stub ran), there is
  no loader to hand over to: the countdown then says `USB boot: let go to cancel` and the gesture goes to the chip's
  USB boot as before. Switch off and on to leave it.
