ChoralRoot 0.19 (in progress)

## Sound

**A cleaner output stage: Options > Output.** The last stage before the headphones, the speaker and the USB
recording has been rebuilt. Until now a full chord with the bass kept the output limiter working all of the time at
full MASTER, which added distortion you could hear as roughness in the mids and highs, and turning MASTER down cost
quiet sounds their detail. The new stage, **Clean**:

- leaves the sound untouched up to just under full level;
- uses the limiter as a safety that holds still instead of moving with every bass note, and that looks ahead: the
  sound waits 0.7 ms, so the limiter has turned a loud peak down before it arrives instead of just after. A hard
  attack is no longer bent on its first millisecond;
- applies MASTER last, so the knob changes the level and nothing else, and a low setting keeps the full resolution.

Measured, full MASTER: on a six-note chord with the bass the distortion drops from about 4 % to between 1 % and
nothing measurable, depending on the sound; on a single note from about 0.5 % to under 0.02 %. What is left on the
loudest chords is the limiter adjusting the level, not a bent wave: the output stage itself adds less than 0.003 %.
A full chord with the bass comes out a little louder than before (up to 1.4 dB), a single note a little quieter
(2.4 dB).

Clean's sound is 0.7 ms late (the limiter's look-ahead; Classic has none). Notes, the arp and the looper are late
together. The metronome's click and, in a USB recording, the chord and bass pairs do not go through the limiter and
are 0.7 ms ahead of it.

**Options > Output** has two values: **Clean** (the new stage, the default in this build) and **Classic** (exactly
the sound of 0.18 and before). You can switch while you play to compare; the change fades over in a hundredth of a
second, without a click. The setting is saved by itself, like the other Options.

Over USB the recording's master pair follows the stage you chose: with Clean it peaks 2 dB under full level, where
Classic stayed about 3.5 dB under it. The chord and bass pairs are unchanged.

## Fixes

**A speaker setting left behind by Felucca.** If your FM-1 ran Felucca with MENU > SPEAKER set to LOWCUT or BASS+
before you installed ChoralRoot, that setting stayed active: every sound lost its low end (up to 17 dB under 100 Hz),
and with BASS+ gained added harmonics. ChoralRoot has no entry for it and Reset Settings did not clear it. 0.19
switches it off for good at the first start and stores that with the next save. A new FM-1, or one that never ran
Felucca with that setting, was not affected.

On 0.17 or 0.18 the only ways to clear it are to restore a backup that does not have it, or to install Felucca, set
MENU > SPEAKER to OFF there, and install ChoralRoot again.

## Going back

0.18 and older read 0.19's settings as their own. They do not know Options > Output; if it is on Classic when you go
back, the older firmware takes its default for one retired setting and nothing else changes.

## CZ-1

**CZ-1 no longer pops when a chord is struck again or changed, and its bass no longer clicks.** Two things in the
engine made a step in the sound, and both are gone.

- A note played again while it still sounded (the same chord struck twice, a chord change that keeps a note, the arp,
  the bass moving from note to note) was cut to silence in an instant and then started over: a pop, a short hole,
  and the attack again. It now carries on from where it was. You will hear the difference beyond the pop: a
  sustained CZ-1 chord struck again swells from its level instead of starting from nothing.
- The timbre envelope (DCW) moved in small jumps 1400 times a second. On a low note with a fast timbre attack each
  jump was audible as a click a few milliseconds after the note began, most of all on CZ BASS and on low piano
  and mallet tones. It now moves smoothly.

Held chords themselves were not at fault in our renders, and nothing inside the engine clips. If you still hear
pops or drop-outs on held CZ-1 chords after this build, it is a different cause (the processor finishing a moment of
sound late) and the unit can say so: run `python3 tools/cz_pop_check.py --read` after playing, or
`--play` to have it play the heaviest CZ-1 chords for 45 seconds and report.

Every CZ-1 tone sounds as it did in level and timbre; the first milliseconds of a note are not sample for sample
what 0.18 played. Saved CZ-1 sounds are untouched. The other engines are unchanged, sample for sample. The cost is
under 1 % for a held chord and about 9 % while a tone's timbre is moving.

Not changed here, and measured for the master's rework: CZ-1 is about 5 dB louder than the other engines for the
same chord (docs/CZ1.md "Level").

## USB recording at 48 kHz

**ChoralRoot In can now also run at 48 kHz, and you switch that on: Options > USB Rate.** Many phones, tablets and
apps only take 48 kHz, and a DAW project at 48 kHz no longer has to convert. This is SLOOP 2.5's feature, which came
from Felucca 1.1.5, made for ChoralRoot's six channels.

- **It is off until you ask for it.** Options > **USB Rate** (after USB Level): `44.1 kHz` (the default) or
  `44.1 + 48 kHz`. At the default the FM-1 presents exactly the audio device 0.18 did, one rate, and the recording is
  bit for bit what 0.18 sent: updating changes nothing about USB audio. Set to `44.1 + 48 kHz`, the computer, phone
  or app chooses (on a Mac: ChoralRoot In's Format in Audio MIDI Setup; on Windows: the device's Advanced
  properties; a DAW from its project).
- **Why an option:** a Mac picks 48 kHz by itself as soon as it is offered, and then every recording pays for the
  conversion, needed or not.
- **Changing it reconnects the FM-1**, as USB Record does: `USB RECONNECTING`, about a second off USB, the MIDI port
  with it. It waits for a backup, a restore or an update that is running (and so does USB Record now). Not during a
  take.
- **At 44.1 kHz nothing changed**, in either setting: the FM-1 itself runs at 44.1 kHz, and so does the headphone
  output.
- **At 48 kHz the FM-1 converts** all six channels on the way out. The treble's top is a little softer (flat to 12
  kHz, 1 dB down at 16 kHz, 2.7 dB at 18 kHz). The conversion takes about 6 % of the processor: in the very heaviest
  sounds (CZ-1 chords with both effects) a voice may drop sooner while a 48 kHz recording runs. Not measured on the
  unit yet.
- With USB Rate at `44.1 + 48 kHz` the computer sees ChoralRoot In as another version of the device (so that it
  reads the two rates instead of remembering one): Windows sets it up again once, a few seconds.

For developers: with the option on, one alternate setting with two discrete rates and the endpoint's `SET_CUR`
(SLOOP's and Felucca's form), `bcdDevice` 3.23, `wMaxPacketSize` 688; with it off, 0.18's descriptors byte for byte
(`bcdDevice` 3.22, one rate, 640; `tests/cr_usb018_desc.h`), `SET_CUR` 48000 taken and without effect, and no
48 kHz code run. The two states differ in `bcdDevice` so that hosts re-read. The option is bit 2 of the settings
block's `pick_roots` (no record version; older records read as off). A 12-tap polyphase FIR x 160 / 147 in the audio
ISR (SLOOP's table); SysEx 79 USB has the rate, the 48 kHz counters and the option's state, `fm1_install.py --debug`
and `tools/uac_check.py --rate` read them (`--rate 48000` against a unit that does not offer it says so);
`tests/cr_usbaudio48_test.c`, `tests/cr_usbrate_test.c`. Flash +5.2 KB, POOL +0.7 KB, RAM +0.1 KB.
docs/USB-AUDIO.md "48 kHz" has the measurements, the cost counted from the unit's listing, and the test plan for
real hosts in both states (one observation so far: macOS chose 48 kHz unasked).

## GLO + ALGORITHM and GLO + PRESETS: the engine, in the layers too

**Hold GLO (OPT) and turn ALGORITHM: the bass engine. Hold GLO and turn PRESETS: the chord engine. Now wherever
the knob alone changes the sound**, not only on the main screen.

- In 0.18 both worked on the main screen (and in the Options) only. In a layer, GLO + ALGORITHM just changed the
  bass sound, as ALGORITHM alone does; before 0.18 it had been the bass level there. Now it is the bass engine in
  the KEY, PERF, FX, BASS, SEQ and LOCK layers, in the view menu, and in the sound editor, with the same picker,
  landing on the sound you last used in that engine. A bass that is off stays off.
- GLO + PRESETS follows the same rule: the chord engine in those layers too.
- **Where a knob has another job, GLO leaves it alone**: in the PLAY menu PRESETS is the loop slot and ALGORITHM the
  record mode; in the loop editor GLO + PRESETS jumps 8 steps and GLO + ALGORITHM sets the Voicing to `Follow` /
  `0`; in the sound editor PRESETS opens the editor's own preset picker.
- In the editor's picker (EDIT held) and on the save dialog GLO changes nothing about the two knobs: both are busy
  with one sound.
- The read-out's bottom line, `Hold OPT to change engine`, now shows wherever that is true.
- The bass level is still BASS held + ALGORITHM. A knob turned while a layer's own button is held past the hold
  time is still that layer's.

For developers: one predicate, `cr_ui.c cu_epk_ok(part)`, for the gesture and for the read-out's hint
(`cu_pool_home` is gone); `tools/emu/scripts/cr_glo_knobs.txt` (32 dumps: every place, the three kept ones, the
held-layer rule, the bass off). The table of places before and after: docs/PRESETS.md.

## The Options and the keys

**The keys keep playing while the Options are open.** Until now the white keys jumped to the first eleven settings
there and nothing sounded, which made it awkward to hear what a setting does: you had to hold a chord before opening
the menu. Now the keyboard is exactly what it was on the view you came from: chords, LOCK, Key Mode, the split, the
arpeggio, LATCH, and a loop being recorded takes what you play. The key lights show what plays; OPT is lit while the
Options are open.

- **To compare Classic and Clean:** OPT, SELECT to Output, play, and turn KNOB 1 while the chord sounds.
- **The menu is SELECT, KNOB 1 and the buttons.** SELECT moves through the settings (every one of them; the keys
  only ever reached the first eleven), KNOB 1 sets the value, OPT, HOME, OCT- or OCT+ leaves. The screen's bottom
  line says so.
- **No key confirms or cancels anything.** Calibrate still starts with OCT+, Reset Settings with OCT+ twice; playing
  while Reset Settings asks `OCT+ again: reset` neither does it nor withdraws the question.
- KNOB 2 to 4, PRESETS and ALGORITHM keep their view jobs in the Options, as before. OCT- and OCT+ close the Options
  there, so the octave is set before you open them.
- Unchanged: OPT in the PLAY menu and in the KEY layer still switches what their keys do and does not open the
  Options; OPT held with a knob or a button is the same second function as before.

For developers: `cr_ui.c` `cu_key_press` has no Options case, `cr_ui_pump` hands the keys to the engine during a
redraw of the Options page, `cr_leds` draws the playing state; the Options screen has a footer. Emulator test
`tools/emu/scripts/cr_opt_keys.txt`.
