ChoralRoot 0.22 (in progress)

- **Chord numerals in Key Mode.** A new setting, **Numerals**, on the KEY layer's second page (hold KEY, turn SELECT,
  KNOB 4). It is off until you turn it on. With Key Mode on, the Chord view then shows the Roman numeral of the chord
  you are playing, in blue above the chord's name: in C major `I` for C, `V` for G, `vi` for Am. Capitals are chords
  with a major third, small letters chords with a minor third. The numeral is the degree you played: Transpose is
  left out, so the tonic chord reads `I` at any Transpose, while the name shows the chord you hear. Hold SAVE to
  keep the setting. Nothing you hear changes.

- **Favorites, with a filter.** Mark the sounds you like, then step through just those. Tap SAVE: in the save menu
  **KNOB 4 marks the sound** (right) or takes the mark off (left); nothing is saved or overwritten. Or mark while
  you browse: in the sound editor turn PRESETS and use KNOB 4 on the sound you are hearing. A marked sound shows a
  small yellow star before its name when you turn PRESETS or ALGORITHM. Factory presets and your own sounds can be
  marked (`00 INIT` cannot).
  - **The filter.** In the save menu **KNOB 2** turns the favorites filter on or off. While it is on, PRESETS and
    ALGORITHM step through the favorites of the engine you are on and nothing else; a yellow line under the meter
    (`favorites 03/07`) shows that it is on. An engine without a favorite steps through all its sounds and says `no
    favorites: all`.
  - **Chord or bass.** **KNOB 3** gives a sound a type: `Both`, `Chord` or `Bass`. With the filter on, Chord sounds
    are offered to the chord part only and Bass sounds to the bass part only. The type stays with the sound when you
    take its mark off and put it back.
  - **The save menu** now works like the Options: SELECT picks one of five entries (Overwrite, Save as new,
    Favorite, Favorites filter, Favorite type) and KNOB 1 changes the selected entry's value. KNOB 1 no longer moves
    between Overwrite and Save as new: use SELECT.
  - The marks, the types and the filter are kept by themselves and are part of a backup.
- **Naming a sound has a cursor.** When you Save as new, SELECT moves a cursor along the name (turned right on the
  last letter it adds one), KNOB 1 changes the letter under it and KNOB 4 to the left deletes it, so a name can be
  written with the knobs alone. The keys still type, just after the cursor. The other knobs do nothing while you name a sound:
  PRESETS and ALGORITHM no longer change the sound under the name, and KNOB 2 no longer changes the last letter
  (that is KNOB 1 at the cursor).

- **Dual Core is on by default.** A new FM-1, and one whose settings you reset (Options > Reset Settings), now uses
  the chip's second core: the bass plays there with its own voices, so a full chord and the bass both sound whole.
  **Updating does not change your setting**: if your FM-1 had Dual Core Off, it still has. To turn it on, set
  Options > Dual Core to On and switch the FM-1 off and on. If your FM-1 ever crashes or restarts while playing,
  set Dual Core to Off and switch off and on.

- **A Single core package, for FM-1s that have trouble with Dual Core.** The web installer offers it under
  **Advanced > Hardware fallback**: the same firmware, built so that the second core is never started. Choose it if
  your FM-1 crashes or freezes with the standard package. Options > Dual Core then reads `1 core build`, and the
  version `ChoralRoot 0.22 1-core`. Your settings, sounds, loops and favorites stay when you switch between the two.

- **FM TONE in the Digitone's ranges.** FM TONE's parameters now read like the Digitone's: 0 to 127 where the
  Digitone counts that way, -64.00 to +63.99 for its centred ones, and two decimals where the Digitone has them
  (cutoff 23.04, HARM, DETUNE, RESONANCE, LEVEL, the LFO speeds and depths ..), so a sound can be compared and copied
  number for number. Imported Digitone sounds keep their decimals.
- **FM TONE sounds saved earlier are converted** when they load (your saved sounds, the factory sounds, the last
  session): their values carried over into the new ranges.
- **The knobs accelerate.** Every knob that edits a value moves its finest step when turned slowly (1, or 0.01 on a
  value with decimals) and up to a twentieth of the range per detent when turned quickly. Lists (presets,
  algorithms, named choices) still move one item per detent.
- **Hold OPT (SHIFT in the editor) to snap.** While you hold it, a value jumps to round numbers: 0, 16, 32 .. on
  0 to 127, 8 steps on -64 to 63, whole numbers on a value with decimals, 8, 16, 32 and 64 on an LFO speed.

## Going back to 0.21

0.21 and the versions before it have no favorites: they ignore the marks, which are still there when you come back
to 0.22 (a backup made on 0.22 restores on 0.21 without them). 0.21 and the versions before it have no Numerals setting and ignore it; nothing else is lost or reset. If you hold
SAVE while on the older version, Numerals is off when you come back to 0.22.

- **128 user sound slots, shared by all engines.** Save up to 128 of your own sounds, of any engine, in any mix (until
  now 32). The slots are numbered 001 to 128. Your 32 sounds from 0.21 are copied into slots 001 to 032 the first time
  the FM-1 starts with 0.22, each with its own patch and its favorite mark.
- **P5 sounds can be saved.** SAVE on a P5 sound now offers Overwrite and Save as new like every engine: the whole
  program is kept with the sound, and loading it brings back exactly what you saved (and its unison).
- **Going back to 0.21** shows the 32 slots as they were when you updated: 0.22 never changes them. Sounds saved
  under 0.22 are not there. If you edit sounds under 0.21 and come back, the device manager's **Import the 0.21
  slots** copies them into 001 to 032 again.
- Backups made with 0.22 hold the 128 slots; installed on 0.21 they restore the settings, the banks and the loops, but
  not the sounds. A 0.21 backup installs into slots 001 to 032.
